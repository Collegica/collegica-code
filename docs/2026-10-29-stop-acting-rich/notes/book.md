## 1. Thesis

- **Aspirationals.** Stanley's term for "people who act rich . . . but actually aren't rich." https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2013/10/leasing-a-popular-substitute-for-wealth/ He also describes them as people who imitate "the glittering rich." In his words, "Generally they have good incomes but relatively low net worths." https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/05/wine-millionaires-versus-the-aspirationals/
- **Glittering rich.** About 80,000 households with a net worth of at least $20M. They really do buy top-end brands, and aspirationals copy them. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/11/makes-of-cars-even-the-glittering-rich/
- **Income is not wealth.** Stanley's line is "income is not net worth, not wealth; wealth is not income." https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/04/stop-acting-rich-the-median-millionaire/ His blog rule "Emphasize net worth; de-emphasize income" makes the same point. There, the typical millionaire's realized income is about 8% (median) of net worth, and they save over 15% of income. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/10/emphasize-net-worth-part-i/
  - The term "balance-sheet affluent" vs "income-statement affluent" comes from *The Millionaire Next Door* (1996). No page I fetched defined it in words. A Stanley post titled "Living Among the Income Statement Affluent" exists but contains no definition. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/04/living-among-the-income-statement-affluent/
- **Expected net worth formula** (from *The Millionaire Next Door*, 1996, not from *Stop Acting Rich*): "Multiply your age times your realized pre-tax annual household income from all sources except inheritances. Divide by ten." This was quoted via Wikipedia, which gives the example that a 50-year-old earning $250,000 should have $1.25M.
  - **PAW** (prodigious accumulator of wealth) = net worth well above that expected figure.
  - **UAW** (under accumulator of wealth) = well below it.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Millionaire_Next_Door (used for orientation only; check against the 1996 book before quoting).
- **Happiness.** The blog reports a national survey of 1,574 high-income/high-net-worth respondents covering 200+ variables. Its headline: those who can "easily afford their consumption lifestyle" are significantly happier than those "acting rich." The top correlates include "I live well below my means" and "We are financially better off than our neighbors." https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/07/to-be-happy-stop-acting-rich/
- **Cars and satisfaction.** Separately, Stanley reports that among "46 makes studied," none had a measurable effect on overall life satisfaction. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/02/cars-more-opinions-than-makes-and-models/

## 2. Specific findings

### Survey base
- Stanley gives the national study as "1,594 respondents." https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/02/cars-more-opinions-than-makes-and-models/
- Another post says 1,574 (https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/07/to-be-happy-stop-acting-rich/). That may be a different subset or a typo. **Use 1,594 and check it against Appendix A.**
- **Survey year (an inference, not stated):** a 2009 post gives "median realized household income from all sources in 2006 was $152,193." That implies fieldwork in 2007, collecting 2006 income. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2009/12/the-low-profile-millionaire-next-door/ I did not find the year stated outright.
- **Pre-crash data.** The survey was taken before the 2008 crash, and the book came out in 2009.

### Cars
- Median price millionaires paid for their most recent vehicle: **$31,367**. For decamillionaires: **$41,997**. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/07/rich-or-drive-rich/ and https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2012/01/drive-rich-or-be-rich/
- "86% of those who drive prestige makes of motor vehicles are not millionaires." https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/07/rich-or-drive-rich/ (Goodreads' summary repeats the 86%.)
- A Wall Street Journal poll cited by Stanley (more than 2,000 respondents): 35% thought being rich requires a car costing $75,000+. Stanley says more than 90% of his millionaires would fail that test. Same URL.
- **Toyota.** *Stop Acting Rich* found Toyota to be "the number one make of motor vehicle most recently acquired by millionaires." Stanley adds that "more than one in ten" drive Toyotas, and "one in four" among millionaire engineers. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/12/toyota-1st-in-quality-and-1-among-millionaires/
- **Leasing.** Only 11.3% of surveyed millionaires leased their latest vehicle. Nearly 1 in 10 millionaires owned a Mercedes, and 86.2% of those bought rather than leased it. By contrast, about half of new Mercedes sales nationally are leases. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2013/10/leasing-a-popular-substitute-for-wealth/
- **Glittering rich.** About half drive a GM full-size SUV (Tahoe/Suburban or a GMC/Cadillac clone). https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/11/makes-of-cars-even-the-glittering-rich/

### Wine (chapter "The grapes of wrath")
- More than 90% of millionaires drink wine.
- Median price per bottle: **$14.54** ($13.09 when bought by the case).
- One in four typically serves wine at $9.93 or less.
- Source: https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/05/wine-millionaires-versus-the-aspirationals/ The post doesn't name its data source. Medium confidence that these are the book's figures.
- **Low-profile segment:** typically pays "just over $10.00" a bottle, and only one in twenty has a wine collection. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2009/12/the-low-profile-millionaire-next-door/

### Watches (chapter "Brother, do you have the time?")
- Median watch price: **$149.50**. About 1 in 5 (19.9%) paid $40 or less.
- Source: https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/08/successful-trial-lawyer-opts-for-40-timex/
- The population is given as millionaires with a $1M+ investment portfolio, which may differ from the main sample. **Verify against the book.**

### Shoes and suits (chapter "Do the shoes make the man?")
- Millionaire women: Nine West is the most-owned shoe brand, with a median maximum price of **$140**. The source is a survey of 1,000 millionaire respondents, possibly a later survey. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2015/02/ask-your-customers-about-their-unfulfilled-needs/
- **Not found:** the men's shoe and suit figures. The posts I fetched gave no men's suit/shoe medians or brands, so those are unverified.
- About 40% of millionaires have clothes altered rather than buying new. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/08/to-the-point-stop-acting-rich/

### Homes and neighbourhoods (chapter "Getting out of the poorhouse")
- Nearly three times as many millionaire households live in homes under $300,000 (**1,138,000**) as in homes worth $1M+ (**403,211**). https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/10/superior-neighborhood-superior-people/
- **"Millionaire Rule 1.49":**
  - Under-$300k homes hold 28.3% of millionaire households; $1M+ homes hold 10%.
  - The median ratio of first-home price to household income was 1.49.
  - Source: https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/11/millionaire-rule-1-49/
  - **Caution:** Goodreads' summary says "most" millionaires live in homes under $300,000. Stanley's own figure is 28.3%. Don't use "most."
- **Productivity by home value:** 100 high-income households in pricey homes produce 65 millionaires, versus 211 among those in homes under $300k. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2012/09/big-house-only-after-big-wealth/
- **Low-profile millionaires:** median home value $293,214, in homes worth $125,000–$395,000. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2009/12/the-low-profile-millionaire-next-door/
- **IRS 2007 estate data** (decedents with $3.5M+ estates): median home $469,021, "less than 10%" of median net worth. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/04/stop-acting-rich-the-median-millionaire/
- **Cost of a status neighbourhood:**
  - Manhattan's housing cost index is 401.5, 4.5 times a Southern city. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/03/manhattan-palo-alto-consider-the-cost-of-living/
  - California and the Northeast have less than half the expected number of millionaires, while the Midwest has 1.65 times the expected number. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2009/12/the-low-profile-millionaire-next-door/
- **Neighbourhood rule** (Goodreads summary only, low confidence): live where your income ranks in the top 20%. https://www.goodreads.com/book/isbn/9780470482551 **Verify in chapter 8.**
- **"Hyperconsumer" share:** not found in any source I could fetch. **Unverified.**

## 3. Practical rules and advice (from Stanley's posts promoting the book)

- Buy a home whose price is easily affordable. Rule 1.49 means a first home at about 1.5× household income. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/11/millionaire-rule-1-49/
- "Buy the toys well after you become wealthy. Not before." This is from a reader case Stanley endorses; his "Option 3" is to own a small multi-unit property and live in one unit. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/02/option-3-for-stop-acting-rich-part-i/
- Buy rather than lease. Use reliability and value as the test, not the badge. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2013/10/leasing-a-popular-substitute-for-wealth/ ; https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/12/toyota-1st-in-quality-and-1-among-millionaires/
- Compare yourself to your own wealth/income group on net worth, not consumption. Give and invest regularly; "invested 10% or more" and "donated 5% or more" are among the happiness correlates. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/07/to-be-happy-stop-acting-rich/
- Choose a low-cost region and neighbourhood. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2011/03/manhattan-palo-alto-consider-the-cost-of-living/
- A reader case Stanley featured: pay off the mortgage, cap new cars at $21,000 and keep them about 10 years, use a Timex. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2009/10/how-to-live-like-a-real-millionaire-part-ii/
- Visible display also carries a security risk (burglary, theft). https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/05/another-reason-to-stop-acting-rich/

## 5. Critiques

- **Survivorship bias.** Nassim Taleb argues the method studies the winners and ignores frugal savers who didn't get rich, and that it ignores the bull market (via Wikipedia, orientation only; the original is in *Fooled by Randomness*). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Millionaire_Next_Door
  - The same applies to *Stop Acting Rich*: frugal habits are observed among people who are already millionaires.
- **Sampling** (my analysis, no external source found). Stanley's samples come from his own mail surveys of high-income/high-net-worth households, reached through neighbourhood targeting. He reports response counts (1,594; 1,000) but no response rates on the pages I saw.
  - Some of his figures are estimates of national counts, such as 1,138,000 households, built by combining his survey with IRS and census data. They are not direct counts.
  - Segments like "glittering rich" and "aspirationals" are his own constructs.
- **Correlation vs causation.** The happiness findings are correlations; Stanley himself frames them as "variation explained" (https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2010/07/to-be-happy-stop-acting-rich/). Low spending may be partly a result of wealth habits, temperament or region, not only a cause of wealth.
- **Era.** The fieldwork was in 2007 on 2006 income, before the crash and before the long rise in house prices after 2012.
  - The $300k home threshold is very different in today's markets. Even Stanley's 2015 post notes San Francisco's median of $691,000. https://themillionairenextdoor.com/2015/01/to-build-wealth-be-careful-where-you-land/
  - That point matters for a Canadian audience in Toronto or Vancouver.
- **Inflation of the threshold.** US CPI-U (CPIAUCSL) averaged 207.344 in 2007 and was 336.298 in September 2026, a factor of about 1.62. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=CPIAUCSL&cosd=2006-01-01 In today's dollars:
  - A 2007 millionaire ≈ $1.62M.
  - The $31,367 car ≈ $50,900.
  - The $41,997 car ≈ $68,100.
  - The $300,000 home ≈ $486,600.
  - Stanley's figures are US nominal dollars, not Canadian.
- **Reader criticism.** The book is repetitive and heavy on brand detail (vodka, watches). This is the Goodreads consensus, low weight. https://www.goodreads.com/book/isbn/9780470482551
