## 2. Tax and account placement

- **Interest is fully taxable.** CRA: interest "must be reported on your return". Foreign interest is converted at the Bank of Canada rate, and a foreign tax credit may be available ([CRA line 12100](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-12100-interest-other-investment-income.html)). That points the 55% bond sleeve to registered accounts (RRSP/TFSA) first.
  - The capital gains inclusion rate stays at **50%**. The planned increase to 2/3 was cancelled on 21 Mar 2025 [snippet] ([Scotia Wealth](https://enrichedthinking.scotiawealthmanagement.com/2025/04/07/cancellation-of-the-proposed-capital-gains-inclusion-rate-increase/)).
- **US withholding, per BlackRock Canada's reference guide** ([PDF](https://www.blackrock.com/ca/investors/en/literature/brochure/withholding-tax-reference-guide-en-ca.pdf)):
  - Holding US-listed ETFs or US stocks directly in an **RRSP/RRIF** is "generally exempt from U.S. withholding tax".
  - "All Canadian-listed ETFs seeking exposure to U.S. bonds are generally exempt" from US withholding on qualified interest income, in any account. That covers both direct holders (ZTL, ZTM) and TLT-wrappers (XTLT, XTLH), "assuming all income generated is qualified interest income".
  - **Practical result:** for the Treasury sleeves, a Canadian-listed ETF avoids the US-withholding question entirely. The direct-holding versus wrapper distinction matters for *equities* (two layers of withholding), not Treasuries.
  - **TFSA:** the treaty exemption (Article XXI) covers retirement plans, not TFSAs. US dividend withholding (15%) in a TFSA can't be recovered [snippet] ([Canadian Couch Potato](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2016/07/11/foreign-withholding-taxes-revisited/); [wealthnorth](https://wealthnorth.ca/investing/tfsa/us-dividend-withholding-tax-tfsa-canada/)). So US-listed PDBC/BCI belong in an RRSP if used at all.
  - **Equity sleeve:** the all-in-one funds (VEQT, XEQT) wrap US-listed ETFs for some foreign stock. In any account that adds a layer of foreign withholding that can't be recovered. That cost is modest at a 30% sleeve. Bender (PWL, now Bender Bender & Bortolotti) covers it in detail ([BBB, US equity ETFs](https://benderbenderbortolotti.com/foreign-withholding-tax-u-s-equity-etfs/), [snippet], since the site returned 403).
- **Physical gold ETFs:** the fund is a Canadian trust, so selling units in a taxable account normally produces a capital gain. Frequent trading risks income treatment [snippet] ([goldstockcanada](https://goldstockcanada.com/blog/capital-gains-tax-on-gold-in-canada-what-you-need-to-know-before-you-sell)). This is a weak, non-CRA source, so the article should hedge. Sprott PHYS's PFIC/QEF point concerns **US** taxpayers only (see 1d).
- **Commodity futures funds (ZCOM, CCOM):**
  - Gains on futures can fall on capital or income account depending on the facts [snippet] ([TaxTips.ca](https://www.taxtips.ca/personaltax/investing/taxtreatment/futures-contracts-and-commodities-futures.htm)).
  - These funds distribute irregularly. CCOM paid $0.5721 on 23 Sep 2026, $1.0905 in Jun 2026 and $0.9178 in Mar 2026 ([CI profile](https://funds.cifinancial.com/en/documents/static/Fund_Profiles/Alternative_Investments/56257/CIAuspiceBroadCommodityFund_Monthly_Profile.pdf)). Their character (income, capital gain or return of capital) arrives on T3 slips. Sales go on T5008.
  - This argues for a registered account, the same as for bonds.
- **Placement sketch** (judgement, not sourced advice):
  - Bonds and commodity futures go in the RRSP/TFSA.
  - Gold and Canadian/global equity are the most tolerable in a taxable account (capital gains, Canadian dividend credit).
  - Any US-listed fund goes in the RRSP only.
  - For a taxable account that must hold bonds, HTB (corporate class, swap) turns interest into a deferred capital gain. It carries the structural risk noted in 1c.
- **Superficial loss rule (taxable rebalancing):**
  - A loss is denied if you or an affiliated person buy "the same or identical property" within 30 days before or after the sale and still hold it after the 30th day. The denied loss is added to the ACB of the new units ([CRA capital losses page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/personal-income/line-12700-capital-gains/capital-losses-deductions.html)).
  - Tax-loss swaps between funds tracking *different indexes* (ZFL on FTSE vs XFLB on Bloomberg; ZGLD vs KILO) are common practice. CRA has not ruled that they are "not identical", so flag this as a judgement call.

- **2022 in product terms:** unhedged ZTL fell −26.6% and hedged ZTL.F −31.5% (chart readings from BMO ETF Facts, as in 1b). In the year long bonds failed, the USD exposure softened the loss.
- **Practitioner commentary:**
  - Bortolotti, 2014: the loonie moves against global equities, so hedging equities *adds* risk for Canadians. He cites J.P. Morgan AM ("produce losses when equities are falling") and CPPIB on the loonie's link to oil and commodities ([Canadian Couch Potato, 6 Mar 2014](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2014/03/06/why-currency-hedging-doesnt-work-in-canada/)).
  - **Counterpoint on bonds:** the same author says "taking currency risk on the bond side is unwise" ([Ask the Spud, 29 Aug 2014](https://canadiancouchpotato.com/2014/08/29/ask-the-spud-should-i-use-global-bonds/)). This is the standard view: hedge foreign bonds, because FX swings swamp bond returns.
  - Bender's "O Canada" post on safe-haven USD is [snippet] only (403) ([BBB](https://benderbenderbortolotti.com/hedging-your-bets-with-currency-hedged-etfs-o-canada/)).
- **For the article:**
  - An unhedged US long-Treasury sleeve (ZTL, XTLT) gives a CAD investor a *double* deflation/crisis hedge: falling yields and a rising USD. The cost is extra volatility in normal times and losses when CAD strengthens (as in 2025).
  - A hedged sleeve (ZTL.F, XTLH) or the Canadian federal sleeve (ZFL) behaves like the textbook All Weather bond leg.
  - A reasonable split: ZFL for the core, with part of the 40% in unhedged ZTL or XTLT.

## 5. Rebalancing practicalities and costs

- **Wealthsimple:** "We don't charge commissions when you buy or sell Canadian or U.S. ETFs". A currency conversion fee applies to USD-listed ETFs ([Wealthsimple ETFs](https://www.wealthsimple.com/en-ca/trade/etfs)). The FX rate itself (about 1.5% is commonly cited) was not verified.
- **Questrade:** sources conflict. One snippet says $0 to buy and sell Canadian and US stocks and ETFs "as of September 2026". Another says ETF buys are free and sells pay the standard commission. questrade.com returned 403 [snippet] ([BrokerChooser](https://brokerchooser.com/broker-reviews/questrade-review/questrade-fees); [yieldmaple](https://www.yieldmaple.com/guides/questrade-fees)). Check before publishing.
- **Bid-ask spreads (12 months to 31 Dec 2025):** ZFL 0.10% ([ETF Facts](https://fundfacts.bmo.com/EtfEnglish/BMO_Long_Federal_Bond_Index_ETF-EN-CAD_Units.pdf)), ZTL 0.22%, ZTL.F 0.40% ([ETF Facts](https://fundfacts.bmo.com/EtfEnglish/BMO_Long-Term_US_Treasury_Bond_Index_ETF-EN-Hedged_Units.pdf)). Smaller funds (XFLB at $31M, ZTM at $98M, ZCOM new) may have wider spreads. Use limit orders.
- **Practical rebalancing:**
  - Rebalance with new contributions and distributions first.
  - Do threshold or annual rebalancing inside registered accounts, where there is no tax friction.
  - In taxable accounts, watch the superficial loss rule (section 2).
  - Using Canadian-listed CAD funds for every sleeve avoids FX conversion costs entirely.
- **Settlement:** KILO settles T+1 ([Purpose](https://www.purposeinvest.com/funds/purpose-gold-bullion-fund)), the Canadian standard since May 2024. The date is general knowledge, not sourced here.
