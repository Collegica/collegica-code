## 2. What they claim about whole life for Canadian corporations

The site speaks in three layers, and the article should keep them apart.

### Layer A: PWG's own written pages (concepts, companions, calculators). Mostly cautious and largely accurate.

- **Corporate-owned insurance** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/concepts/corporate-owned-insurance): the corporation owns the policy and pays premiums; "projected dividends are not guaranteed." Says plainly that "corporate TFSA" is an analogy, "not the name of a registered corporate savings account." Advises asking for "guaranteed and projected values separately, the cost of stopping early, and an explanation of the advisor's compensation." **Accurate and fair.**
- **Accessing cash value** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/concepts/accessing-policy-cash-value): a withdrawal "may produce taxable income." A policy loan "can be taxable when it exceeds the relevant adjusted cost basis." A bank collateral loan is "a separate debt." Asks what happens "if interest rates rise." **Accurate.**
- **CDA** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/concepts/cda): the death benefit adds to the CDA "generally after subtracting the relevant adjusted cost basis," and an excessive election "can trigger substantial tax." **Accurate.**
- **Passive income and the small business deduction** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/concepts/retained-earnings-drag and the tools): the business limit falls between $50,000 and $150,000 of AAII; associated corporations count. The calculator gets the arithmetic right: $5 of business limit lost per $1 of AAII over $50k, federal 9% to 15%, so $75k AAII leaves a $375k limit and costs $7,500 more in federal tax. It also uses Ontario's 50.17% initial rate on investment income and the 30⅔% refundable portion (https://preservewealthgroup.com/tools/canada-business-limit, https://preservewealthgroup.com/tools/ccpc). It says outright that the result "does not prove this tax can be avoided." **Accurate**; still to be checked line by line against CRA.
- The consultation FAQ says to ask your advisor about "fees, compensation and other costs" (https://preservewealthgroup.com/canada).

### Layer B: advisor-supplied case studies. Promotional, numbers-heavy, each with a "limitations" paragraph.

All carry the disclaimer that figures are "illustrations based on the advisor's own case design rather than booked results."

- **Alberta, $200k+/yr for 10 years** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-alberta-multi-company-200k-exempt). Benefits named:
  - a "Close to 50%" tax rate on passive income before the plan
  - growth "Not taxed annually" and "does not feed the $50,000 passive income grind"
  - access "by loan or withdrawal"
  - the death benefit paid out through the CDA "largely tax-free"
  
  Limitation stated: it only works with surplus the business doesn't need, an insurable owner and a CCPC.
- **B.C. commodities brokerage, $3M over two years** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-commodities-brokerage-3m-ifa). Claims:
  - growth at a "5-6% participating dividend rate, not taxed annually"
  - the bank treated the cash value as cash collateral for a ~$2M line of credit
  - "Cash value is not passive income"
  
  This is the **immediate financing arrangement (IFA) / leverage** use case. Limitation stated: "if the lender changes its terms or rates move," the borrowing stops working.
- **B.C. tech exit** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-bc-tech-exit-1m-portfolio). Drew ~$1M on a policy-secured line of credit at the market bottom and bought Canadian bank shares. Claims:
  - "Net cost of those shares $0"
  - "Policy growth through it all 5-6%," "because the bank lent the money, not the policy"
  
  Limitation stated: entry "usually starts around $500K to $1M of premium," and "he still owed the bank every dollar." This is **leveraged investing with survivorship bias**: the case was picked because the trade worked.
- **N.S. advisor's own policies** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-financial-services-ns-400k-liquidity). Four participating whole life policies were "built for cash, not coverage," funded about 15 years to $523,000 of cash value. He borrowed $400,000 by policy loan: "No forms, no credit check," with $290,000 arriving in 3 days. The subject is the advisor himself. Limitation stated: a policy "in its second or third year has almost nothing to order against." This is the classic **"be your own bank" / infinite-banking design** (minimum death benefit, maximum deposits up to the exempt limit). The video title is "He Borrowed $400K Tax-Free to Save His Daughter's Life."
- **N.S., $120k/yr for 10 years** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-business-owner-4-of-96). Claims:
  - a $2,789,000 death benefit from year one, with a "$2,640,000 Capital Dividend Account credit"
  - "$22,000 a year, tax-free ... through policy loans he never has to repay"
  - a year-20 value of $2,874,000 against $2,300,000 invested personally "at 7 percent"
  - an anecdote that an accounting firm reviewed 96 such strategies and found only 4 "properly structured"
  
  **Internal inconsistency:** the summary says "Total in $1,500,000," but $120,000 × 10 = $1,200,000.
- **Ontario succession** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-family-business-transition-4m-tax-to-350k). Estate freeze plus donating preferred shares plus corporate-owned insurance is projected to cut tax from $4M to ~$350k (a "91%" drop). The case labels these "projections from the case design." Note the planning is multi-part; insurance is only one piece.
- **Ontario security company** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-security-services-7k). A salary/dividend review saves $7,000 a year, and the case mentions "$404,000 of projected cash value in two corporate PAR policies by year five." **Internal inconsistency:** the headline stat says "Tax bill cut 9%"; the body says "a 7% cut," and $7k on $100k is 7%.
- **Glazing business** (https://preservewealthgroup.com/case-studies/ca-commercial-glazing-70k-kept-liquid). **The most balanced case on the site.** The advisor replaced a proposed $100k/yr whole life premium with a $30k/yr Empire Life participating policy plus $70k/yr of flexible corporate-class investing. It even says "The submission does not establish that the two insurance proposals provided identical coverage." **This is a counter-example worth citing: an advisor scaling the insurance back.**
- **Target audience:** incorporated owners "Keeping $100,000 or more in your corporation each year" (https://preservewealthgroup.com/canada). The advisor form screens for "Canadian high-tax-paying business owners."

## 3. What they do not disclose

- **Commission.** No figures anywhere: not the advisor's first-year commission on a whole life premium, nor bonuses or MGA overrides. The current site only says "ask your advisor." The archived 2025 FAQ admitted that consultants are paid by the insurance companies. There is also no disclosure of **how much each advisor pays PWG per introduction**, or whether the fee is tied to sales. That is a conflict a reader cannot measure.
- **Surrender charges and early-years cash value.** Two hints appear: the Fyon transcript's "break even ... at year six" and the "year two or three" caveat. But no case shows a year-by-year guaranteed cash surrender value against premiums paid. "Surrender charge" never appears on any Canadian page; it shows up only on U.S. annuity pages and the Cox episode.
- **Illustration assumptions.** The 5–6% / 6.35% figures are dividend scale interest rates (DSIRs), not the return on premiums. No case says which insurer's DSIR was used, the "current scale less 1%" alternative scale, the mortality and expense charges, or what the internal rate of return on premiums would be.
- **Dividend scale not guaranteed.** Stated on the concept pages, but **not** in the case study headline numbers ("5-6% participating dividend rate") or in the transcripts.
- **Leverage risk.** The B.C. cases admit that lender terms and interest rates can change. Missing: what happens if the loan plus interest exceeds the collateral (forced repayment or policy lapse, which creates a taxable disposition); the interest rate actually charged; whether interest is deductible; and the leveraged-insurance rules (ITA 20(1)(e.2) for collateral insurance, and the 2013 federal budget's ban on "10/8" and leveraged insured annuity arrangements).
- **"Loans he never has to repay."** Not explained: compounding loan interest reduces the death benefit paid to the estate, and if the policy lapses with loans outstanding there is a tax bill.
- **Tax-rule risk.** Missing: the 2017 exempt-test changes; the 2016 changes to CDA credits for policy transfers and the ACB/NCPI treatment; the possibility of future changes to the passive-income or CDA rules. The 2025 site called the strategy "explicitly endorsed by CRA."
- **Opportunity cost and fair comparisons.** The $120k case compares the policy with a 7% personal investment taxed at personal rates. It does not compare it with a corporate investment portfolio built on capital gains and eligible dividends (refundable tax, CDA from capital gains). The $0-net-cost bank-share trade ignores the policy premium and the risk taken.
- **Suitability for smaller owners.** The site targets $100k a year of retained earnings, while one advisor says entry for the leverage structure "usually starts around $500K to $1M of premium."
- **Underwriting and premium-commitment risk.** Mentioned, briefly (two cases mention insurability).
- **Who verified the case numbers.** The cases are "supplied by financial professionals" and "advisor-reported." PWG does not say it checked them.

## 4. Claims to check against primary sources

- [ ] The exempt test: what "funded right up to the CRA exempt limit" means; ITA s.148, Reg. 306; the 2017 changes.
- [ ] Growth in an exempt policy is not taxed annually, and is not AAII for the business limit (ITA 125(5.1)(b), 125(7) definition of AAII). Do dispositions of the policy or policy loans above ACB create AAII?
- [ ] Policy loan above ACB is a taxable disposition (ITA 148(9) "proceeds of the disposition"). This contradicts Kelly's "because it's a loan, there's no tax implications" for insurer policy loans; it is true only for third-party bank collateral loans.
- [ ] CDA credit = death benefit minus the policy's ACB (ITA 89(1)(d)). Check the $2,789,000 death benefit vs $2,640,000 CDA credit in the $120k case.
- [ ] Capital dividend election (Form T2054) and the Part III tax on excessive elections (60%).
- [ ] The SBD grind: $5 per $1 of AAII over $50k, eliminated at $150k (ITA 125(5.1)); 9% vs 15% federal; provincial treatment (Ontario and New Brunswick don't follow the grind).
- [ ] Ontario 50.17% initial tax on investment income; the 30⅔% refundable portion; Alberta's "near 50%" (likely 46.67%); BC small-business vs general rate (Munier's "12 to 27").
- [ ] Current Canadian participating whole life DSIRs (Canada Life, Sun Life, Equitable, Empire, iA, Manulife), 2025–2026, compared with the "5-6%" and "6.35%" figures quoted.
- [ ] Typical bank lending on whole life cash surrender value (claimed "90 to 100%"; commonly lower), and what triggers a margin call or collateral shortfall.
- [ ] Deductibility of interest on collateral loans, and the collateral insurance premium deduction (ITA 20(1)(e.2)); the 2013 rules on leveraged insurance and annuities.
- [ ] Taxable benefit / shareholder benefit when the corporation guarantees a shareholder's personal loan ("guarantee fee" of 1–2%, ITA 15(1), 80.4).
- [ ] Creditor protection for corporate-owned policies in Canada (generally limited; protection depends on the beneficiary class under provincial Insurance Acts, and a corporate-owned policy is usually an asset of the corporation).
- [ ] CRA "endorsement": CRA does not endorse products. The 2025 site's "explicitly endorsed by CRA" and "audit-proof" are unsupportable.
- [ ] Whether PWG's referral fees and advisor commissions are disclosed as required under provincial insurance conduct rules (e.g. CCIR/CISRO Fair Treatment of Customers guidance; FSRA rules for life agents' disclosure of conflicts and compensation, including referral arrangements).
- [ ] Licence status of each named advisor on the provincial registers (ICBC, AIC, NS OSI, FSRA, AMF). Not done; see Access.
- [ ] Wyoming Secretary of State record for Preserve Wealth Group LLC (formation date, registered agent).
- [ ] Internal inconsistencies: $1.5M "Total in" vs 10 × $120k; "9%" vs "7%" in the security case.

### Fair-minded summary for the article

PWG does not sell insurance itself. Its written educational pages and calculators are measured and mostly correct. They state that dividends aren't guaranteed, that policy loans can be taxable, that the CDA is not cash, and that the "corporate TFSA" is only an analogy, and they tell readers to ask about compensation. The aggressive claims sit in the advisor-supplied case studies (leverage, "tax-free" loans "never repaid," DSIRs quoted as if they were returns) and above all in the podcast titles and transcripts ("TFSA on Steroids," "without a downside," "no-brainer," infinite banking). The firm is paid by the advisors it promotes, its Trustpilot reviews come from those advisors, and its 2025 site made claims ("complete tax elimination," "endorsed by CRA," "audit-proof") that it has since quietly removed.
