# Whole life inside the company — plan and research

Plan and notes for a Finance article on corporate-owned participating whole
life insurance in Canada, prompted by preservewealthgroup.com, a referral site
marketing it to business owners. Started 2026-10-27. Draft:
`website/finance/corporate-whole-life/index.md` (`draft: true` until reviewed).

Audience: incorporated owners being pitched the "corporate TFSA". Same stance
as *Fixed or Variable*: arithmetic, not advice.

## Decisions

1. **About the product, not the firm.** The site is the occasion and gets one
   section, "Reading the pitch": its claims checked against the rules, quoted
   without naming individual advisors, with its accurate pages credited.
2. **No home-made policy model.** A par policy cannot be reproduced without an
   insurer's illustration; the article uses the insurers' and analysts' own
   published IRRs and cash values, and does only transparent arithmetic on
   them (the SBD grind, the pre-tax-equivalent division).
3. **Plain language**, following the reader feedback on the desktop-AI piece.
4. **Nothing about "infinite banking" enforcement.** No regulator or CRA
   document names it; the article says so.

## Added 2026-10-27: how the money reaches the family

A section on the path from death benefit to heirs (company → capital dividend
→ estate → will), the deemed sale of shares at death, and an anonymous worked
example: a sole Ontario shareholder with a six-year-old heir and an online
will. The example is a real reader's situation, used with permission and
stripped of names, places, services and dates. Its legal points rest on
ITA s. 70(5) and 70(5.3) (policy valued at cash surrender value at death) and
s. 112(3.2) (stop-loss on capital dividends, partial relief for estates),
both read on Justice Laws 2026-10-27, plus Ontario's estate administration
tax (about $15 per $1,000 above $50,000). The example is not a review of
anyone's will; the article says what to ask a lawyer.

## Open, before publishing

- No advisor's licence was verified on a provincial register (registers are
  JavaScript or 403 to scripts). The article says we could not confirm any.
- Ontario's non-application of the grind, and BC's application, rest on
  secondary sources (medium confidence).
- Sun Life and Manulife 2026 DSIRs are from broker reports, not the insurers'
  pages (403). The article quotes only the 6.00–6.40% range, whose ends
  (Canada Life, Equitable) were read directly.
- Vanishing-premium settlement dates and amounts not confirmed; the article
  gives neither.
- PWL's white paper itself was not readable (403); figures are from the
  Rational Reminder show notes.
