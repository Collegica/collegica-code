## 1. How participating whole life works (Canada)

### Mechanics
- **Guaranteed values.** A par policy guarantees the base premium, the base death benefit and the base cash value. Dividends come on top of that.
  - Canada Life lists "Guaranteed base premiums" and "Guaranteed base payout on death". Source: Canada Life, 2025 Financial facts (combined open par account), https://www.acp.canadalife.com/content/dam/advisors/documents/marketing/insurance/en_ca/par/Canada-Life-Financial-facts.pdf
- **What the DSIR is.** The dividend scale interest rate (DSIR) is the smoothed investment return on the assets backing par liabilities.
  - It "doesn't include the return on assets backing the participating account surplus."
  - It is "only one of many factors" in policy performance. Mortality, expenses, taxes and lapses also enter. (Same Canada Life source.)
  - Equitable gives the same list: "mortality, expense, lapse, claims experience, taxes". It adds that dividends are "paid at the sole discretion of the Board of Directors." Source: https://cdn.equitable.ca/forms/unsecured/insurance/1505.pdf
  - An independent broker report states that DSIRs "are not guaranteed and are not a policy's rate of return." Source: A Better Broker, Sept 2026, https://abetterbroker.ca/resources/par-whole-life-report-2026
- **DSIRs are not comparable across companies.** Canada Life says a DSIR "should not be assumed to be comparable" across companies, because asset mix and smoothing differ. Source: Canada Life historical brochure (May 2025), https://www.canadalife.com/content/dam/canadalife/documents/insurance/par-whole-life-insurance/en/46-11488Z_FF_CL_Historical-PAR-Financials_Brochure_AODA_FINAL.pdf
- **Scale of the par business.**
  - The Canada Life open par account held $62.8B at Dec. 31, 2025, across about 1.4M policies.
  - It paid $1.8B in policyowner dividends in 2025.
  - Shareholders received 2.5% of par account profits, which was $52M. (2025 Financial facts.)
- **Dividend options.**
  - Cash
  - Premium reduction
  - Accumulation on deposit (the interest is taxable annually)
  - Paid-up additions (PUA), which buy more permanent insurance and raise both cash value and death benefit
  - Enhanced coverage, a term/PUA blend
  - Loan repayment
  - Source: PolicyAdvisor, Jan 2026, https://www.policyadvisor.com/life-insurance/what-are-dividends-in-whole-life-insurance/
  - Empire's "Enhanced Coverage" option carries a "Lifetime Guarantee". The guarantee is lost if you take policy loans, surrender PUAs or change the dividend option. Source: Empire Life case study, June 2026, https://www.empire.ca/document/14421
- **Premium offset ("vanishing premium").** Dividends are used to pay the premiums after a set number of years.
  - When rates fell, clients had to resume paying. This led to class actions (see section 5).
  - IE describes the original pitch: premiums for "eight to 12 years," after which dividends would carry the policy **[snippet]**. Source: https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry-news/canada-life-reaches-agreement-on-premium-offset-lawsuits/ (the page now returns 404)
- **Illustration rules (CLHIA Guideline G6, 1996, last amended 2009).**
  - Guaranteed and non-guaranteed values must be distinguished.
  - At least one less favourable scenario must be shown alongside the primary one.
  - Illustrations "must not imply in any manner that non-guaranteed values are guaranteed."
  - Sources: https://www.clhia.ca/web/CLHIA_LP4W_LND_Webstation.nsf/page/7B785D7C7342484C8525784F0058BD17/$file/G6.pdf **[snippet]**; Jeff Cait, Investment Executive, Apr 14 2026, https://www.investmentexecutive.com/perspective/columns/jeff-cait/the-illustration-standard-we-promised-in-1996/
  - Cait notes that G6 governs presentation only. It assumes the product has already been chosen.

## 2. Costs, incentives, disclosure and regulation

### Commissions
- **Rational Reminder ep. 221** (Oct 6 2022, PWL/Ben Felix):
  - First-year compensation is about **50–200% of the first annual premium**.
  - On an $8,240 premium, that is $4,120–$16,480.
  - The show notes call this "not transparent" to clients.
  - Source: https://rationalreminder.ca/podcast/221
- **MoneySense** (Courtney Reilly-Larke, Mar 24 2021):
  - First-year commission "usually ranges from 40% to 60% of the annual premium."
  - The MGA/production bonus is negotiated on top.
  - In LSM's example, a $1,794 whole life premium paid $986.70 FYC plus a $1,184.04 bonus, or $2,170.74 in total. That is about 121% of the first premium.
  - Source: https://www.moneysense.ca/spend/insurance/life-insurance/how-life-insurance-brokers-get-paid/
- **Broker/industry sites** **[snippet]**:
  - Whole life FYC of 50–100% of target premium, up to about 120% in bonus tiers.
  - Renewals of 2–5% in years 2–10.
  - Overrides of 100–180% of base commission.
  - Sources: https://lsminsurance.ca/life-insurance-canada/2013/08/are-life-insurance-commissions-on-their-way-out; https://www.insuranceandestates.com/life-insurance-agent-commission/
- **Large cases.** The Globe (Mark McGrath, Feb 9 2026) says "six-figure commission cheques" await sellers of complex IRP/leveraged plans. Source: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/globe-advisor/insurance/article-why-the-secrets-of-the-wealthy-are-often-just-an-insurance-sales-pitch/

### Regulatory framework
- **CCIR/CISRO Incentive Management Guidance** (released Nov 30 2022; complements the Sept 2018 FTC Guidance). The appendix lists risky incentive features:
  - "Bonus rates that increase with predetermined sales volumes thresholds"
  - "Commissions linked to the premium level or the investment amount"
  - Contests and trips
  - "Incentive paid in advance of the service expected"
  - Chargebacks that push advisers to keep clients in unsuitable products
  - Sources: https://www.ccir-ccrra.org/Documents/View/3690; https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/ccir-and-cisro-release-new-guidance-on-incentive-management-under-the-fair-treatment-of-customers-836618107.html
  - FTC Guidance: https://www.ccir-ccrra.org/Documents/View/3377
- **FSRA thematic reviews of MGAs** (released Oct 2023; tiered-recruitment MGAs Greatway, WFG and Experior):
  - 80% of universal life files did not show alignment with customer needs.
  - 130 agents examined; 65 faced enforcement; 184 contraventions.
  - 21 agents received monetary penalties.
  - FSRA warned that the effects "can be catastrophic, resulting in policy lapse and loss of all premiums paid."
  - Source: Investment Executive, Oct 3 2023, https://www.investmentexecutive.com/industry-news/review-of-multi-level-marketing-mgas-results-in-enforcement-for-65-life-agents/
- **FSRA on overfunded UL sold as savings.** It found money used "under the guise of helping them grow their savings." That money would have been better spent on "TFSAs or by reducing their personal debt."
  - Fines totalled $224,000.
  - The findings also cite undisclosed conflicts and misleading illustrations.
  - Source: Money.ca via Yahoo, Jul 1 2026, https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/ontario-fines-life-insurance-agents-063500439.html
  - Note: this concerns UL sold to modest-income clients, not corporate par whole life. It is still the clearest Canadian regulator statement against selling insurance as a savings product to people who have not used TFSA/RRSP room.
- **FSRA's Life Agent & MGA Licensing Suitability guidance** (consultation from Nov 21 2023). Investment Executive covered it Mar 11 2024. Source: https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news-newspaper/fsras-proposed-guidance-on-life-agent-suitability-a-good-first-step/
- **FSRA's proposed MGA rule** (Nov 2025). Source: https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2025/11/proposed-life-and-health-managing-general-agents-rule **[snippet]**
- **FSRA enforcement, fiscal 2024–25:**
  - 27 life-sector sanctions
  - 13 licence revocations or refusals
  - $232,000 in administrative monetary penalties
  - Misconduct reports up 22%
  - Source: Investment Executive, Mar 30 2026, https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/ontario-insurance-sector-second-most-active-for-fsra-enforcement/
- **Earlier FSRA/CCIR review** (Sept 28 2022). FSRA questioned UL sold widely to modest-wealth clients and whether "sales incentives are aligned with customer needs" (paraphrase). Source: https://www.advisor.ca/insurance/life/universal-life-shouldnt-be-universally-recommended-regulator-says/
- **Standard of care.** Insurance agents work under a suitability standard, not a best-interest standard (McGrath, Globe, link above).
- **"Infinite banking."** No regulator statement naming it was found (see Access section). A pro-IBC Canadian site itself concedes that enforcement has focused on "describing insurance as an investment." **[snippet]** Source: https://ibcfinancial.com/objections-and-risks/is-it-legitimate/ (a seller's site; weak source).

### Leveraged insurance and tax authorities
- **10/8 arrangements.** These were legislated out in Budget 2013 (Mar 21 2013; Bill C-4, Royal Assent Dec 12 2013). Leveraged insured annuities (LIAs) were curtailed at the same time. Sources: https://www.advisor.ca/industry-news/industry/should-clients-drop-10-8s/ **[snippet]**; https://advisor.manulife.ca/advisors/insurance/tax-retirement-and-estate-planning/technical-planning-support/triple-back-to-backs-before-march-21-2013.html **[snippet]**
- **CRA news release on LIAs** (Aug 26 2020). CRA said it would apply GAAR. Tim Cestnick, Globe, Oct 29 2020, quotes CRA that the insurance and annuity are "heavily interdependent." Source: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/personal-finance/taxes/article-cra-takes-offence-with-leveraged-insurance-products/
- **Honey v. The King, 2026 TCC 170.**
  - Two brothers in 10/8 arrangements through RBC credit facilities claimed 10% interest deductions for 2009–2013.
  - The Tax Court upheld CRA's reassessment, which allowed only 4.18%.
  - Source: David Rotfleisch, Mondaq, Oct 20 2026, https://www.mondaq.com/canada/tax-authorities/1857224/can-the-cra-limit-your-interest-deduction-a-canadian-tax-lawyers-analysis-of-honey-v-the-king

## 3. Independent and pro-insurance analyses, with numbers

### Critical or independent
- **PWL Capital / Rational Reminder ep. 221** (Oct 6 2022). Case: a healthy 40-year-old at the 2022 top Ontario rate, after-tax compound returns.

  | Case | Horizon | After-tax return | Comparison |
  |------|---------|------------------|------------|
  | Whole life, female | 40 years (death at 80) | 4.83% | stocks 5.09% |
  | Whole life, female | 60 years (death at 100) | 2.12% | stocks 5.18%, bonds 2.15% |
  | Whole life, male | 40 years | 4.18% | |
  | Whole life, male | 60 years | 1.66% | bonds 2.15% |

  - Term-to-100 beat whole life at every horizon.
  - At the current scale minus 1%, the par policy's death benefit at 90 is about equal to a cheaper guaranteed policy.
  - Corporate vs personal ownership: "not a whole lot different" (Felix, per the show notes).
  - Sources: https://rationalreminder.ca/podcast/221; the PWL white paper "Permanent Life Insurance" (Cecereu, Tarasoff, Felix, Sept 7 2023), https://pwlcapital.com/permanent-life-insurance/ (403; content not verified).
  - The white paper argues that insurance sold as an investment is "often related to conflicts of interest" **[snippet]**.
- **Mark McGrath (Globe, Feb 9 2026)** on IRPs:
  - The agent picks the assumptions: rates of return, tax rates, mortality age and loan rates.
  - One reviewed case was 30% below projection only 10 years into a 50-year plan.
  - In a "death spiral," the loan reaches the bank's loan-to-value limit and the bank calls it.
  - Link above.
- **Mike Morgan, RBC Wealth Management** (Sept 24 2026). This is a balanced worked case.
  - Paul, 50, pays $100k/yr for 10 years into a corporate par policy.
  - He takes $75k/yr in collateral loans from 65 to 84.
  - Illustrated: death benefit at 90 of $6.35M; family receives $2.58M; CDA room $3.57M; **IRR on death benefit 5.31%** at 90.
  - The "pre-tax equivalent" claimed is 11.74%, or 13.62% counting CDA. It ranges from 9.79% (death at 100) to 14.33% (death at 70), against a corporate passive rate of 50.17%.
  - **Stress tests:**
    - A loan rate 2 points higher (5.45% to 7.45%) cuts the family's amount from $2.58M to $1.01M. Loan-to-value hits 96% by 90 and breaches the 90% cap at 88.
    - Guaranteed cash value at 90 is $1.4M, against $5.6M illustrated.
    - "About three quarters of what the bank is lending against is dividends."
    - Zero cash value in years 1–4.
  - Suits only clients with RRSP/TFSA full, years of excess corporate cash, and a real permanent need.
  - Source: https://wmc.rbcwealthmanagement.com/morgan-wealth-management/blog/insured-retirement-plan-in-canada:-how-corporate-owned-life-insurance-works-6855011249652797932
- **Jeff Cait (IE, Sept 8 2026).**
  - Case: a 37-year-old man, $90k coverage, 20-pay at $1,233/yr.
  - Guaranteed IRR on death benefit: 2.92% at death at 90 (median), 2.63% at 95, 2.48% at 98.
  - He compares this with FP Canada-style planning assumptions of 3.2% for fixed income and 6.3–6.4% for equities.
  - Source: https://www.investmentexecutive.com/perspective/columns/jeff-cait/the-certainty-of-life-insurance/
- **MoneySense "Ask" column** (Feb 7 2019). Recommends doing a full financial plan before buying permanent insurance, and notes the drag from taxes in non-registered accounts. Source: https://www.moneysense.ca/columns/ask-moneysense/should-i-invest-my-money-or-buy-a-life-insurance-policy-instead/

### Pro-insurance (insurer and seller material)
- **Empire Life case study** (illustrated June 16 2026):
  - A couple aged 70, $100k/yr for 8 years, joint last-to-die Optimax Wealth.
  - IRR on death benefit falls the longer they live: **6.95% (death in year 10), 4.98% (year 15), 4.63% (year 20)**.
  - At the current scale minus 1%: 4.29% (year 15) and 3.88% (year 20).
  - Guaranteed: 3.94% (year 15) and 2.74% (year 20).
  - The headline "tax-equivalent" 12.64% assumes both die by year 10 and a 45% tax rate, against a fixed-income-only alternative.
  - **Cash surrender value:** $71,670 after year 1 (on $100k paid). Breakeven comes in **year 12**.
  - Source: https://www.empire.ca/document/14421
  - Useful as a primary-source example of how "pre-tax equivalent" headlines are built: early death, a fixed-income comparator and a high tax rate.
- **Wealthi (broker; Wenting Hu, updated Jul 2026).**
  - Case: a 43-year-old male, $100k/yr, 20-pay, "classic" design.
  - Year 1 cash value $51,693 (about 52% of premium).
  - Breakeven before year 10 ($1.16M at year 10, on $1M paid).
  - About 3.3% tax-free by year 20; about 4.8% by 85.
  - The "early cash value" design has 94% liquidity in year 1 but a lower long-run value.
  - Pre-tax equivalents claimed: 10.4% for interest, 5.6% for deferred capital gains.
  - Source: https://wealthi.ca/families-retirement/participating-whole-life-insurance/
  - Note: the capital-gains equivalent (5.6%) is the more honest benchmark for an equity investor, and it is far below the interest-income figure.
- **HealthQuotes (Sept 11 2026).** Net cash value IRR is typically 3–5%. Cash value grows "very slowly in the first 7–12 years." Source: https://healthquotes.ca/blog/whole-life-policy-good-investment-canada
- **Search-snippet IRRs.** A par estate-value IRR of about 2.97–3.3% at 85 appears in advisor material **[snippet]**. Source: https://protectyourwealth.ca/investment-grade-participating-whole-life-insurance-in-canadian-businesses/

### Corporate tax mechanics that favour insurance (fair to both sides)
- **SBD grind.** A CCPC loses $5 of its $500k small-business limit for each $1 of passive income over $50k. Growth inside an exempt policy is not counted toward that passive income. Sources: Advisor.ca (Michael McKiernan, Oct 4 2023), https://www.advisor.ca/insurance/life/the-pros-and-cons-of-corporate-owned-life-insurance/; BDO, https://www.bdo.ca/insights/passive-investment-income-impact-small-business-deduction **[snippet]**
- **CDA.** The death benefit minus the policy's ACB is credited to the capital dividend account and can be paid out tax-free.
  - The ACB grinds down over time, so the CDA credit is smaller early in older owners' policies.
  - MD's example: a $1M death benefit with $133,691 ACB gives an $866,309 CDA credit.
  - Sources: Advisor.ca above; https://invested.mdm.ca/capital-dividend-account-cda-and-corporate-owned-life-insurance-2/
- **Cons of corporate ownership** (Advisor.ca above):
  - Policy cash value can taint the "small business corporation" test for the lifetime capital gains exemption.
  - No creditor protection.
  - The ongoing premium is a cash-flow risk.
  - Complications on sale of the business.
- **Exempt test 2017.** The test's maturity assumption moved from age 85 to 90 **[snippet]**. Source: https://www.northlandwealth.com/the-artisan/exempt-life-insurance-tax-changes-canadian-families

## 4. Who it suits and who it doesn't

**Suits (consensus across critics and sellers):**
- RRSP and TFSA are maxed.
- The financial plan is fully funded and corporate surplus will never be spent.
- There is a genuine permanent need: estate liquidity for capital gains at death, equalisation, or charitable or second-generation legacy intent.
- Conservative investor who would otherwise hold fixed income.
- Corporation passive income is near the $50k SBD threshold.
- Sources: RR 221; RBC (Morgan); Advisor.ca; Wealthi.

**Doesn't suit:**
- Might need the money back in the first decade.
  - Year-1 surrender is about 52–72% of premium (Wealthi, Empire).
  - Zero cash value in years 1–4 in RBC's design.
  - Breakeven at about year 10–12.
- Tight cash flow, or a 10–20-year commitment it can't sustain (Wealthi).
- Unused RRSP/TFSA room, or high-interest debt (FSRA findings; Wealthi).
- Pure temporary protection need; term costs about one-tenth as much (Wealthi; RR 221).
- Risk-tolerant equity investors. RR 221 shows stocks beating whole life after tax at 40- and 60-year horizons.
- Those relying on leverage: loan-rate and dividend-scale risk (RBC stress test; Globe).
- Insurability: rated or uninsurable people pay more, and this changes the IRR. Not quantified in the sources found.

**Lapse statistics:**
- **US (LIMRA 2011, cited in Gottlieb & Smetters, Wharton, 2016):**
  - Traditional whole life lapses about 3.0% a year (3.7% by face amount).
  - Almost 25% of permanent policyholders lapse within 3 years, and 40% within 10.
  - About 88% of UL policies never pay a death claim (Milliman 2004).
  - CIA data on 11 Canadian insurers show young policyholders lapse almost three times as often as older ones.
  - Source: https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Insurance41.pdf
- **RR 221:** "only about 31%" of whole life policies with $500k+ face remain after 30 years (source data unspecified; likely US).
- **Canadian pricing:**
  - Products are partly lapse-supported (RGA's David Pelletier, quoted in the same paper).
  - Jeff Cait (Advisor.ca, Mar 23 2026): industry lapse assumptions are about 1–2% a year, and an early exit "costs the pool more than it contributes" for about five years.
  - Source: https://www.advisor.ca/insurance/life/the-math-of-life-insurance-pt-1/

## 5. Complaints, settlements, enforcement

- **Vanishing premium / premium offset class actions** (par whole life sold in the 1980s and 1990s on illustrated dividends):
  - **London Life:** a class of about 500,000 policyholders (1980–95) settled for **$180M** **[snippet]**. Sources: CBC, https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/london-life-settles-vanishing-premium-lawsuit-for-180-million-1.270212 (403); Globe, https://www.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/london-life-settles-vanishing-premium-suit/article25706099/; https://classactionlaw.ca/completed-claims/london-life/press-release/
