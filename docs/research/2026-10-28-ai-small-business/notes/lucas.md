## The claims and advice, in order

1. **Opening claim (opinion):** anyone in real estate without AI "by the end
   of 2027" will be like an agent without a cell phone today, and "never ...
   able to catch up".
2. **How she learned:** 18 months of YouTube after a 2023 Gary Vaynerchuk
   event. Advice now: put many YouTube videos into "what used to be called
   NotebookLM, it is now called Gemini Notebook" and ask it what you need to
   know first.
3. **Train the AI before using it:** "take 15 to 30 minutes. Have it interview
   you"; upload emails, blogs, messages so it learns context and "tonality".
   Without context and constraints output is "very basic and very vague".
   Host's analogy: you wouldn't hire a graduate and give no training.
   Let it ask clarifying questions instead of guessing.
4. **Quick win:** have AI go through email and texts for "open loops",
   missed leads, unanswered questions, drafts never sent — "reactivation of
   business that's already there".
5. **Find what customers ask:** have AI search Reddit threads; AnswerThePublic
   ("three free searches a day"); answer those questions as the expert.
   Talking to everybody means "really not talking to anybody".
6. **Your database beats new leads:** 100 people who know you vs millions who
   don't; have AI find who opens, clicks, watches; which posts get shares and
   saves; e.g. referrals vs open houses — "double down on what's working".
7. **Don't automate chaos:** "automating chaos is just going to cause more
   chaos"; build a foundation first. Host: AI amplifies the good and the bad.
8. **AI slop:** generic AI images "hurting consumer trust". Instead: AI writes
   one-or-two-sentence answers in your voice, overlaid on your own real
   footage ("B-roll"), with a caption — shareable and savable.
9. **Speak to the customer, not peers:** investors make content for other
   investors; instead address the distressed homeowner, probate, divorce,
   with empathy. Example: a Massachusetts investor posts answers over a video
   of stairs and closes probate deals nationwide.
10. **The funnel:** a lead magnet (Zoom, community event, "five mistakes to
    avoid") → ManyChat "comment the word X" → automatic DM with the item →
    second call to action: book with a self-serve calendar → intake form →
    CRM nurtures. Claims: "over 200 consultations booked ... in the last 12
    months and 187 of those people actually showed up".
11. **Cost:** host and guest: "like 40, 50 bucks a month"; host pays for both
    ChatGPT and Claude ( each).
12. **Not replacing yourself:** automate the tedious to show up better in
    person. "You do not need to be techy"; ask the free version "how can you
    help me?".
13. **Build in phases:** she added the calendar after the lead magnet got no
    conversions, then the form, then CRM automation: "every problem that
    you're solving with AI should create another problem" — the next task.
14. **Models mentioned:** compares the same prompt across Anthropic's and
    OpenAI's latest (transcribed as "Fable"/"Fathom" and "Astra").
15. **Weekend quick wins:** (a) upload your deal-analysis spreadsheet, "This
    is how I analyze a deal", then type an address and numbers for a yes/no;
    (b) pull the top 5–25 names from email/texts and reach out this weekend;
    (c) connect AI to Gmail, calendar, Canva, CRM so it can email, schedule
    and even book a restaurant.
16. **Rapid fire:** hire an admin before a salesperson; first hire, a buyer's
    agent, "create[d] more admin work". Biggest time saver: have AI look at
    your current systems and "get as lean as possible" before adding more.
