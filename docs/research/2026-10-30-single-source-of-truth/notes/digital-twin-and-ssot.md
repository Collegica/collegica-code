## Definitions

- **Digital twin** (Wikipedia): a computational model of a real product,
  system or process. By the strict definition it "continuously uses real data
  from its physical counterpart" to stay synchronised; a model without that
  link is described as an "overly broad and largely marketing-oriented"
  interpretation. NASA's 2010 work is given as the first practical
  definition.
  - **Use in the article:** the term is avoided (owner's decision). What a
    small business can build is a written record of how it works, kept
    current by people and an assistant; not a live, sensor-fed twin.
- **Single source of truth** (Wikipedia): structuring information so that
  "every data element is mastered (or edited) in only one place". Copies may
  exist, but only the master is updated, or copies need reconciling.
  - **Use in the article:** the core idea. One place where each fact is
    written; everything else (a website FAQ, a quote, an AI's answer) reads
    from it.

## Statistics Canada: AI use by businesses

Canadian Survey on Business Conditions, second quarter of 2025. Collected
1 April to 5 May 2025, from business establishments with employees.

- **12.2%** of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services in
  the previous 12 months, up from **6.1%** in Q2 2024.
- By industry, highest: information and cultural industries (35.6%),
  professional, scientific and technical services (31.7%), finance and
  insurance (30.6%). Lowest: accommodation and food services (1.5%);
  agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting (1.8%); transportation and
  warehousing (1.8%).
- Among AI users, the most common applications: text analytics (35.7%), data
  analytics (26.4%), virtual agents or chatbots (24.8%), marketing
  automation (23.1%), large language models (19.1%).
- **Plans to adopt AI software in the next 12 months:** 17.9% of all
  businesses; construction 9.6% (8.0% a year earlier).
- Most AI users reported little change in employee tasks or headcount.

## Construction, for the later version (secondary only)

- **ISO 19650** (Wikipedia): a family of international standards for
  information management using BIM across a built asset's life cycle. Six
  parts: concepts (Part 1, 2018), delivery (Part 2, 2018), operation (Part 3,
  2020), information exchange (Part 4, 2022), security (Part 5, 2020),
  health and safety information (Part 6, 2025). Developed by ISO/TC 59/SC 13
  from the UK's PAS 1192 series. Amendments to Parts 1–3 under way in 2026.
- The **common data environment** (CDE) is ISO 19650's single source of
  truth for a project. Its states (work in progress, shared, published,
  archived) are the construction equivalent of the example wiki's draft and
  verified pages. *Not read in the standard itself; check before the
  construction version relies on the details.*
